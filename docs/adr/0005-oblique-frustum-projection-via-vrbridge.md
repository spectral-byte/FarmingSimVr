# Oblique Frustum Projection via VRBridge

## Context and Decision
VR headset optical displays exhibit asymmetric fields of view (nasal vs. temporal and top vs. bottom) due to lens canting, IPD adjustment, and optical center alignment. Rather than attempting low-level D3D12 constant buffer or vertex shader projection matrix patching—which is fragile across engine updates—the native Injector extracts per-eye optical field of view tangents ($tanLeft, tanRight, tanUp, tanDown$) from OpenXR's view state (`XrFovf`), translates them into vertical FOV and tan-angle offsets ($xOffset = \tan(\theta_R) + \tan(\theta_L)$, $yOffset = \tan(\theta_U) + \tan(\theta_D)$), and publishes them through the in-process `VRBridge` table. The Companion Mod consumes the vertical FOV each frame to configure `setFovY`.

**Symmetric projection is the only projection.** The Companion Mod keeps the camera on the optical axis (`setProjectionOffset(camNode, 0, 0)`) for both eyes, in both Stereo Presentation Modes, and the Injector submits a matching symmetric FOV to OpenXR: a vertical FOV sized to cover the widest side of the headset FOV (below), and a horizontal half-angle derived from the eye swapchain aspect ratio ($\tan(\theta_{x}/2) = aspect \cdot \tan(\theta_{y}/2)$). Because the rendered image and the submitted FOV describe the same symmetric frustum, the compositor reprojects it without stretching or perspective swim, and parallel rays at infinity avoid cross-eyed disparity shear.

**The symmetric FOV covers the widest side of the headset FOV.** Headset lenses are asymmetric, and a symmetric frustum cannot follow them, so it is sized to include every edge of the headset's own FOV rather than to match its total span. Its vertical half-angle tangent is the largest of the headset's up tangent, its down tangent, and its wider horizontal tangent (left or right) divided by the eye swapchain aspect ($\tan(\theta_{y}/2) = \max(\tan\theta_{U}, \tan\theta_{D}, \max(\tan\theta_{L}, \tan\theta_{R}) / aspect)$); the horizontal half-angle still follows from the aspect as above. Before the eye swapchains exist the aspect is the headset FOV's own, and it switches to the swapchain aspect once they are created. The Injector's eye projection is the single source: the vertical FOV VRBridge publishes (`fovY`, with the matching `fovX`) is what the Companion Mod hands the engine camera, and the FOV the Injector submits at xrEndFrame is the same one, so rendering and submission still agree exactly. The headset's own tangents and tan offsets (`tanLeft`, `tanRight`, `tanUp`, `tanDown`, `xOffset`, `yOffset`) keep reporting the headset FOV. The trade-off changes from lost coverage to pixel density: the same Render Resolution is spread over a wider view, which leaves the nasal and lower sides of each eye rendered but never shown. The Injector logs the headset's per-eye FOV and the rendered FOV, so a report shows both.

**FOV Overscan widens it further.** The player can render a margin beyond the covering FOV (`vrFovOverscan`, 0 to 20 percent), so a frame the VR runtime turns to follow the head still covers the display. The covering tangent is multiplied by $(1 + percent / 100)$: tangents, not angles, so both axes widen by the same factor and the pixels stay square. It is applied inside the Injector's eye projection, so the engine camera and the submitted frame still describe one frustum; `fovY` and `fovX` publish the widened FOV and the headset tangents are unchanged. A new value is latched when the next OpenXR frame begins, so both eyes of a pair, in either Stereo Presentation Mode, share one FOV. It does not apply while a menu or 2D Floating Window Mode is shown. The cost is pixel density, as above.

**The Oblique Frustum was removed.** It had been an opt-in (the `allowObliqueProjection` setting and the `vrObliqueProjection` console command) that applied the published `xOffset`/`yOffset` per eye. The opt-in never matched what the Injector submits: the engine rendered an off-axis frustum while the Injector told the OpenXR runtime the view was symmetric, so the compositor showed a skewed image. The setting, the console command and the projection offset scale are gone. A settings file that still holds `allowObliqueProjection` loads without error, the entry is ignored and it is not written back. The VRBridge eye projection still publishes the runtime's tan offsets, and camera records still carry their projection offset fields (now always zero); the bridge contract is unchanged. Bringing the Oblique Frustum back needs a design that submits the frustum the engine actually renders, plus a headset round to confirm the engine's projection offset units.

## Status
Accepted, amended: Oblique Frustum removed; symmetric FOV sized to cover the widest side of the headset FOV.

## Considered Options
- Direct D3D12 constant buffer matrix patching (rejected: multithreaded command recording creates high risk of descriptor/constant buffer race conditions and patch desynchronization).
- Symmetrical field of view approximation with post-process shader distortion (rejected: causes geometric distortion and perspective shear during head motion).
- Tan-angle offset extraction from OpenXR and native `setProjectionOffset` via VRBridge, applied per eye (originally selected as the mechanism; withdrawn when the Oblique Frustum was removed, because the submitted FOV never described the rendered frustum).
- Aspect-matched symmetric FOV submitted to OpenXR with a centered engine projection (selected: submitted FOV and rendered frustum agree exactly). Originally the vertical FOV was the headset's vertical span, which left the outer and lower sides of asymmetric lenses short; it is now the widest side of the headset FOV.
